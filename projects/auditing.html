Mandatory Third-Party White-Box Auditing

policy · ai governance

This is a timed policy proposal I wrote for the IAPS fellowship application which I'm proud of.

An evaluation gap is emerging with general-purpose AIs — that is to say, “performance on pre-deployment tests does not reliably predict real-world utility or risk” (International AI Safety Report 2026). This gap is constantly widening due to the rapid scaling of capabilities. Simultaneously, advanced models are demonstrating “alignment faking” and the ability to distinguish between a test environment and a live deployment, allowing them to bypass safety filters by hiding their true capabilities until released. This is currently not problematic due to chain-of-thought monitoring being effective, but there’s no guarantee that advanced models won’t be able to obfuscate their reasoning or begin reasoning in an incomprehensible language.

I therefore propose the use of mandatory third-party, white-box auditing for the following reasons:

  1. Black-box testing is becoming less trustworthy as the evaluation gap grows and situationally aware models become more prevalent. Internal safety teams and external auditors currently rely heavily on behavioral testing — judging a model by the text or actions it outputs. This is dangerous as deceptively aligned models are incentivized to output the exact benign response as a truly safe model during a test. White-box techniques such as sparse autoencoders have the capability to analyze models’ internal reasoning as they produce an answer, effectively verifying causal correctness rather than just surface-level persuasiveness.
  2. Corporate safety teams face commercial pressures to quickly release the most capable models. Allowing frontier labs to self-certify their models entirely by themselves is a conflict of interest. This has been cited as one of the reasons for Mrinank Sharma’s resignation as a safety researcher at Anthropic, saying they “constantly face pressures to set aside what matters most.”
