Maryland AI Safety Initiative

organization · leadership

The field of AI safety is constrained by talent. Only 2% of technical AI research is safety-related, despite nearly 58% of AI researchers believing in a non-trivial (>5%) chance of existential threat. Given that universities are full of intellectually curious people figuring out what to work in, they're an incredible place to build pipelines to fill this gap. AI safety groups have had a lot of success at other unverisities, and with our proximity to DC (policy) and strong computer science program (technical research), I found UMD well-positioned to start a group.

Thus, I applied for the Pathfinder fellowship and founded the Maryland AI Safety Initiative (MASI) this past winter. My tentative goal was to create a community for people interested in AI safety and guide them in their careers through fellowships and research opportunities. In our first semester, we recieved $4000 in funding, ran an intro fellowship of 15-20 active participants, sent 8 students to 4 different AI conferences, and are soon to fund a student research project. We plan to expand next semester with heavier professor involvement, a more polished fellowship program, and a structured research accelerator. I hope to release a retrospective blog soon!
